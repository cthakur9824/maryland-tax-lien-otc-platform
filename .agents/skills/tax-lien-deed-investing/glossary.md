# Investor's Glossary of Tax Lien & Tax Deed Terms

**Accelerated Sale** — A tax lien sale where the county sells the current year’s unpaid property taxes instead of waiting until the following year, leaving no prior-year delinquent taxes attached to the lien. (Ch 1)

**Adjudicated Property** — Real estate that failed to sell at a tax sale and was transferred/struck off to the municipality or parish to hold in trust. (Ch 4, Ch 5)

**Affidavit of Notice** — A sworn legal statement filed by an investor or county confirming that all required constitutional and statutory pre-sale notices were served on interested parties. (Ch 6, Ch 10)

**Assessed Value** — The monetary valuation placed upon real estate by the county tax assessor for local property taxation purposes, typically lower than fair market value. (Ch 3)

**Assignment of Certificate** — The legal endorsement and transfer of an existing tax lien certificate from the county or an investor to a new purchaser. (Ch 4)

**Automatic Stay** — An injunction governed by § 362 of the U.S. Bankruptcy Code that automatically freezes all tax foreclosures, sales, and collection actions upon a debtor's bankruptcy filing. (Ch 7, Ch 10)

**Barment (Foreclosure of Equity of Redemption)** — Statutory legal procedure (notably in Georgia) terminating the former owner’s right to redeem a tax deed property after 12 months. (Ch 6, Ch 9)

**Bid-Down Interest** — An auction format where the opening bid is the statutory maximum interest rate (e.g., 18%), and bidders compete by bidding *down* the interest rate they are willing to accept. (Ch 5)

**Bid-Down Ownership** — An auction format where bidders bid down the percentage of undivided property equity/ownership they will accept for paying 100% of the back taxes. (Ch 5)

**Cash for Keys** — A negotiated agreement offering an occupant a cash incentive ($500–$2,000) to voluntarily vacate a foreclosed tax deed property in broom-clean condition without court eviction. (Ch 6, Ch 10)

**Certificate of Sale** — A preliminary statutory document issued to a winning bidder certifying the purchase of a tax lien or redeemable tax deed. (Ch 1, Ch 5)

**Cloud on Title** — Any claim, encumbrance, or defect (including unserved heirs or procedural notice flaws) that impairs the marketability of real estate title. (Ch 6)

**Commissioner’s Sale** — A secondary auction in states like Indiana where unsold tax liens are re-offered with reduced minimum bids and accelerated 120-day redemption periods. (Ch 5, Ch 9)

**Contract for Deed (Land Contract)** — A seller-financing agreement where the buyer makes monthly payments toward ownership while the investor retains legal title until full payoff. (Ch 6)

**Deed in Lieu of Foreclosure** — A voluntary conveyance where the delinquent owner signs over property title directly to the lien holder to avoid formal court foreclosure. (Ch 6)

**Due Diligence Funnel** — A systematic 4-stage screening process (desktop filters, GIS analysis, title/lien verification, on-site physical check) to eliminate junk parcels. (Ch 3)

**Ejectment / Writ of Possession** — A legal court action and sheriff execution order to remove unauthorized occupants or former owners from a tax deed property. (Ch 6, Ch 10)

**FEMA Flood Map** — Federal geospatial database showing 100-year and 500-year flood plain hazard boundaries (Zone A, AE, VE) impacting property insurability. (Ch 3)

**First Lien Priority (Super-Priority)** — The statutory doctrine establishing property tax liens as senior to private mortgages, deeds of trust, and mechanic's liens. (Ch 1)

**Forfeiture** — The statutory process where unredeemed delinquent land title transfers to the state or county government for public disposition. (Ch 4, Ch 9)

**GIS (Geographic Information System)** — County interactive digital map showing exact parcel boundary lines, acreage, aerial photos, and zoning classifications. (Ch 3, Ch 8)

**Immediate Settlement Rule** — Local auction rule (e.g., Harris County, TX) requiring winning bidders to pay immediately in certified funds before the next parcel is called. (Ch 2)

**In Rem Foreclosure** — A judicial proceeding directed against the physical property itself rather than against the individual property owner. (Ch 6, Ch 9)

**IRS 120-Day Redemption Right** — Federal statutory right under 26 U.S.C. § 7425 allowing the IRS 120 days post-tax sale to redeem property encumbered by a federal tax lien. (Ch 3, Ch 7, Ch 10)

**Lands Available for Taxes** — Florida's statutory repository of unsold tax deeds available for immediate over-the-counter purchase at face value plus statutory fees. (Ch 4, Ch 9)

**Lease Option** — An agreement combining a residential lease with an upfront option consideration fee granting the tenant the right to purchase the property at a fixed price. (Ch 6)

**Marketable Title** — Title to real property that is clear of clouds and defects, enabling title insurance companies to issue standard owner policies to retail buyers. (Ch 6)

**Over-The-Counter (OTC)** — Direct purchase of unsold, seasoned tax liens or deeds from county inventory without auction competition. (Ch 4)

**Parcel Identification Number (PIN / APN)** — A unique numerical identifier assigned to a specific parcel of real estate by the county tax assessor. (Ch 2, Ch 3)

**Premium Bidding** — An auction format where bidders bid *up* the cash purchase price in dollar increments above the opening delinquent tax amount. (Ch 5)

**Property Use Code (DOR Code)** — Standardized classification code designating property type (e.g., 0100 = Single Family, 0200 = Multi-Family, 1000 = Vacant Land). (Ch 3)

**Quiet Title Action** — A formal civil lawsuit filed in circuit court to extinguish all prior ownership claims and cloud defects, establishing unassailable legal title. (Ch 6, Ch 10)

**Redeemable Tax Deed** — A recorded deed issued to an auction buyer that conveys title subject to the owner's right to redeem within a statutory period by paying a high flat penalty. (Ch 1, Ch 9)

**Redemption Period** — The statutory timeframe (typically 6 months to 3 years) during which a delinquent property owner can repay back taxes plus interest/penalties to retain ownership. (Ch 1)

**Repository List** — Pennsylvania's registry of unsold delinquent properties available for direct over-the-counter purchase free and clear of all prior municipal and private liens. (Ch 4, Ch 9)

**Self-Directed IRA (SDIRA)** — A retirement account allowing the account holder to invest in alternative assets (tax liens, deeds, real estate) with 100% tax-deferred or tax-free compounding. (Ch 7)

**Special Assessment District (CDD / SID)** — Local governmental bond assessments levied against specific properties to fund road, water, or community infrastructure. (Ch 3, Ch 10)

**Statutory Floor (e.g., Florida 5%)** — A mandatory minimum statutory return paid to the tax lien holder upon redemption, regardless of how low the rate was bid down at auction. (Ch 5, Ch 9)

**Struck-Off Property** — Real estate that received no bids at a sheriff tax sale and was transferred to the taxing authority to hold in trust. (Ch 4, Ch 9)

**Subsequent Taxes ("Subs")** — Future annual taxes on a parcel where an investor already holds a prior tax lien; the investor can pay them to earn statutory interest without bidding. (Ch 1, Ch 10)

**Tax Deed** — A legal document transferring physical ownership of delinquent real estate directly from the county to the auction winner. (Ch 1)

**Tax Deed Application (TDA)** — The formal process in Florida where an unredeemed lien holder after 2 years applies to force the parcel to public tax deed auction. (Ch 5, Ch 9)

**Tax Lien Certificate (TLC)** — A first-position debt certificate representing unpaid property taxes, earning statutory interest until redeemed or foreclosed. (Ch 1)

**Tax Title Services (TTS)** — Specialized private certification firm that audits county foreclosure notice procedures to qualify tax deeds for standard title insurance without litigation. (Ch 6)

**Tax-to-Value Ratio (TVR)** — Total delinquent taxes divided by fair market value; a ratio under 15% indicates significant protective equity. (Ch 3, Ch 8)

**Upset Bid Period** — A statutory post-sale window (e.g., 10 days in North Carolina) during which any bidder can reopen the auction by submitting a bid at least 5%–10% higher. (Ch 5, Ch 9)

**Upset Sale** — The initial tax sale in Pennsylvania where the property is sold subject to existing mortgages and senior municipal liens. (Ch 9)
