# Chapter 5: Auction Execution — Live and Online Platforms

## Core Idea
Auction execution requires mastering both live courthouse sales and major online platforms. Winning profitably demands strict adherence to bidding mechanics (Premium, Bid-Down Interest, Rotational), pre-auction registration rules, and mathematical bidding caps that eliminate emotional overbidding.

---

## Frameworks Introduced

- **The 4 Bidding Mechanism Models**:
  1. **Premium Bidding (Most Deed States & select Lien States - e.g., CO, IN, CA, TX)**:
     - *Mechanics*: Opening bid starts at delinquent taxes + fees. Bidders bid *up* in dollar increments. Highest cash bidder wins.
     - *Lien Nuance*: In some lien states (e.g., CO), interest is earned only on the base tax lien amount, *not* on the premium paid above face value.
  2. **Bid-Down Interest Bidding (e.g., FL, AZ, NJ, IA)**:
     - *Mechanics*: Opening bid starts at statutory maximum interest (e.g., 18% in FL, 16% in AZ). Bidders bid *down* the interest rate they are willing to accept.
     - *Example*: Bidding drops from 18% → 15% → 10% → 3% → 0.25%. The lowest interest bidder wins the certificate.
     - *Florida 5% Mandatory Floor Rule*: In Florida, regardless of how low the rate is bid down (even to 0.25%), the certificate holder receives a mandatory minimum 5% penalty return when redeemed!
  3. **Bid-Down Ownership / Percentage Undivided Interest (e.g., LA, NE)**:
     - *Mechanics*: Bidders bid down the percentage of undivided property ownership they are willing to accept in exchange for paying 100% of the delinquent taxes (e.g., 100% → 99% → 50% → 1%).
  4. **Rotational / Round-Robin Bidding**:
     - *Mechanics*: Bidders are called in numerical sequence from a registration roster. Each bidder can accept the next available lien at the statutory rate or pass to the next attendee.

- **Major Online Auction Platforms**:
  - **RealAuction.com**: Primary portal for Florida, New Jersey, Arizona, Colorado, and Maryland county tax sales. Supports proxy bidding, dynamic countdown extensions, and automated batch uploads.
  - **Bid4Assets.com**: Major platform for California, Washington, and Michigan tax deed sales. Specializes in forfeited land auctions with escrow closings.
  - **CivicSource.com**: Dominant platform for Louisiana adjudicated tax property and Tennessee tax sales.
  - **GovEase.com**: Key online auction platform for Alabama, Mississippi, Georgia, and Missouri counties.
  - **ZeusAuction / SRI Services (sri-taxsale.com)**: Primary manager of Indiana, Michigan, and Ohio commissioner and tax certificate sales.

- **Live Auction Day Tactical Protocol**:
  1. *Funds Structure*: Bring cashier's checks drawn in split denominations ($1k, $5k, $10k, $25k) to accommodate exact winning bids without stranding excess capital.
  2. *The Max Bid Ceilings Rule*: Calculate maximum allowable purchase price (MAO) *before* arriving at the courthouse. Write the exact number in ink next to each parcel on your catalog sheet. Never exceed this figure by a single dollar.
  3. *Physical Route Drive-By*: Conduct drive-bys 24–48 hours prior to sale. Map an efficient loop to visually verify roofline, occupancy, and neighborhood trends on your top 20 candidate parcels.

## State-Specific Auction Case Studies

| State | Sale Type | Statutory Max | Bidding Type | Redemption Period | Key Strategic Nuance |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Florida** | Tax Lien | 18% | Bid-down to 0.25% | 2 Years | Mandatory 5% floor rule on redemptions; Tax Deed Application (TDA) after 2 years. |
| **Arizona** | Tax Lien | 16% | Bid-down to 1% | 3 Years | Highly digitized; institutional competition on tier-1 parcels; strong secondary OTC market. |
| **Colorado** | Tax Lien | Formula (Treasury + 9%) | Premium Bidding | 3 Years | Premium does not earn interest; investor can request treasurer's deed after 3 years. |
| **Indiana** | Tax Lien | 10%–15% Penalty | Premium Bidding | 1 Year | Unsold liens move to Commissioner's Certificate Sales with reduced 120-day redemption! |
| **Louisiana** | Tax Title | 12% + 5% Penalty | Bid-down % ownership | 3 Years | Adjudicated property sales offer direct discounted acquisitions via CivicSource. |
| **Maryland** | Tax Lien | 6%–24% (County specific) | High-bid Premium | 6 Months | Shortest redemption window; investor must file formal judicial foreclosure in circuit court. |
| **New Jersey** | Tax Lien | 18% | Bid-down to 0% + Premium | 2 Years | Fierce institutional bidding down to 0% with large premiums; high yields come from subsequent taxes. |

## Mental Models

- **"Auction Rooms are Built on Emotional Contagion"**: The fast cadence of an auctioneer is designed to trigger fear of missing out (FOMO). An investor must act like an unemotional execution algorithm.
- **"Proxy Bidding as a Discipline Shield"**: Online proxy bidding allows you to submit your pre-determined maximum mathematical bid and walk away, preventing real-time emotional bidding wars.

---

## Anti-patterns & Common Pitfalls

- **Bidding Past Your Underwritten Maximum**: Getting caught in an ego contest with another bidder and bidding away your entire profit margin.
- **Neglecting Pre-Sale Redemption Drops**: Failing to download the updated auction catalog 1 hour before the sale. Up to 40% of listed properties redeem in the 48 hours leading up to auction!
- **Overpaying Premium on Zero-Interest Assets**: Paying large premiums on tax liens without realizing the premium produces 0% return upon quick redemption.

---

## Key Takeaways

1. Pre-register and fund auction escrow accounts at least 7–14 days in advance for online sales.
2. In bid-down interest states like Florida, the 5% statutory floor provides downside yield protection even on low bid-down rates.
3. Download the final updated parcel catalog the morning of the auction to avoid bidding on redeemed or pulled parcels.
4. Set strict mathematical maximum bid thresholds before the auction begins and never deviate during live bidding.
