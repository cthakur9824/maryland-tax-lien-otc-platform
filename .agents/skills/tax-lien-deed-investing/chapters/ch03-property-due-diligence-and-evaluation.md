# Chapter 3: Property Due Diligence & Physical Evaluation

## Core Idea
Never buy a tax lien or tax deed based solely on an address or assessed tax value. Thorough due diligence separates lucrative investments from catastrophic liabilities (such as landlocked slivers, retention ponds, or condemned toxic sites). A systematic multi-step vetting funnel filters thousands of delinquent list parcels down to a curated list of high-conviction targets.

---

## Frameworks Introduced

- **The 4-Stage Due Diligence Funnel**:
  ```
  [Stage 1: Bulk Desktop Filter]
       ├── Eliminate sub-$2,000 assessed values (often slivers/common areas)
       ├── Filter by Property Class Code (0100=Single Family, 0200=Commercial, etc.)
       └── Check zoning (Residential/Commercial vs Conservation/Agricultural)
  [Stage 2: Geospatial & GIS Analysis]
       ├── Overlay county GIS parcel boundaries (confirm shape, road frontage, acreage)
       ├── Check topography, wetlands, and FEMA 100-year flood plain zones
       └── Inspect Google Maps / Street View / Bing Aerial for physical structure & roof condition
  [Stage 3: Legal & Encumbrance Screening]
       ├── Search county recorder for superior liens (Municipal water/sewer, demolition orders)
       ├── Check for IRS Federal Tax Liens (120-day super-redemption right)
       └── Confirm bankruptcy docket status (automatic stay voids sales)
  [Stage 4: On-Site Physical Inspection (Deeds / Primary Lien Targets)]
       ├── Drive-by inspection (occupancy status, roof sag, foundation cracks, neighborhood pride)
       └── Renovation estimate matrix (roof, HVAC, windows, cosmetic scope)
  ```

- **The "Junk Land" Elimination Checklist (Instant Disqualifiers)**:
  - *Sliver Lots / Spite Strips*: 2-foot-wide strips between two subdivisions or road right-of-ways.
  - *Subdivision Common Areas*: Retention basins, retention ponds, golf cart paths, entrance signs assessed separately.
  - *Landlocked Parcels*: No deeded ingress/egress easement or public road frontage.
  - *Environmental Hazards*: Former gas stations, dry cleaners, auto scrap yards, brownfields (strict liability under CERCLA/Superfund laws).
  - *Condemned / Red-Tagged Structures*: Properties with active municipal demolition orders where the city will bill the investor $10k–$30k for teardown.

- **Renovation Cost Estimation Formula (Quick Heuristics)**:
  - *Light Cosmetic (Paint, carpet, fixtures)*: $15–$25 / sq ft
  - *Medium Rehab (Kitchen/bath update, flooring, paint, minor drywall)*: $30–$45 / sq ft
  - *Heavy Rehab (New roof, full HVAC, electrical rewiring, plumbing, drywall)*: $50–$80+ / sq ft
  - *Key Structural Inspection Areas*:
    1. *Roof*: Shingle curling, moss, structural sagging.
    2. *Foundation*: Stepped diagonal mortar cracks, bowing basement walls.
    3. *HVAC / Mechanicals*: Missing outdoor A/C compressor (theft target on vacant homes).
    4. *Plumbing / Electrical*: Stripped copper pipe, legacy knob-and-tube or Zinsco/Federal Pacific panels.

## Key Concepts

- **GIS (Geographic Information System) Mapping**: County online interactive maps showing exact parcel boundaries, dimensions, aerial imagery, zoning overlays, and utility connections.
- **Property Use Code (DOR / Assessor Code)**: Standardized county classification code indicating property type (e.g., 01 = Single Family Residential, 08 = Multi-family, 10 = Vacant Residential, 40 = Commercial).
- **Assessed Value vs Fair Market Value (FMV)**: Assessed value is the county tax assessor's valuation for tax calculation (often 50%–80% of true market value). Always use recent comparable sales (comps) rather than assessed value for underwriting.
- **Surviving Liens**: Specific encumbrances not extinguished by tax foreclosure:
  - *Federal Tax Liens (IRS)*: 120-day right of redemption from the date of the tax deed sale.
  - *Municipal Utility & Code Liens*: City water, sewer, weed abatement, and demolition liens (varies by state).
  - *Special Assessment Liens (CDD / SID)*: Community development district bonds for infrastructure.
- **Occupancy Status**: Determining whether the property is owner-occupied, tenant-occupied, squatter-inhabited, or vacant. Vacant homes carry vandalism risks; occupied homes require formal eviction proceedings.

---

## Mental Models

- **"Assessed Value is a Tax Anchor, Not Market Value"**: Never use the tax assessor's number to determine profit margins. Look at recent neighborhood closed comps.
- **"The 10-Foot Rule for Vacant Land"**: If a piece of vacant land is less than 25 feet wide or has no street access, assume it has zero standalone market value.
- **"Assume Worst-Case Interior"**: When bidding on occupied or boarded tax deeds where interior inspection is prohibited, underwrite the rehab budget assuming a total gut down to the studs.

---

## Anti-patterns & Common Pitfalls

- **Bidding on Retention Ponds**: Buying an attractive 1-acre parcel that is actually the stormwater runoff basin for a neighboring shopping center.
- **Ignoring Demolition Liens**: Winning a $1,500 tax deed only to receive a $20,000 city invoice for emergency building demolition completed two weeks earlier.
- **Skipping IRS Search**: Buying a property subject to an IRS lien without verifying whether the IRS will redeem the parcel within 120 days.
- **Falling in Love with High Yields on Junk Parcels**: Buying 18% liens on useless underwater marshland where the owner will gladly abandon the property and let you inherit the tax liability.

---

## Key Takeaways

1. Use county GIS maps to verify parcel shape, square footage, road frontage, and exact physical location before bidding.
2. Filter out non-buildable slivers, utility easements, and retention basins using DOR property classification codes.
3. Check for municipal code violations, water/sewer liens, and active demolition orders at the city clerk's office.
4. Underwrite property values using recent MLS/Zillow closed comps, subtracting full rehab costs, closing expenses, and holding fees.
