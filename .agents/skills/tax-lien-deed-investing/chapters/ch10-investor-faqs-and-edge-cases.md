# Chapter 10: Investor FAQs & Statutory Edge Cases

## Core Idea
Navigating complex statutory nuances, boundary legal conditions, and edge cases prevents costly capital mistakes. This chapter provides direct, decision-ready answers to the 40+ most critical operational, legal, and tax questions faced by tax lien and tax deed practitioners.

---

## High-Impact Legal & Operational FAQs

### 1. Fundamental Mechanics & Rights
- **Q: Does buying a tax lien certificate give me the right to enter, inspect, or lease the property?**
  - **A: NO.** A tax lien is strictly a debt instrument (first-position statutory mortgage). You have zero rights of possession, trespass, or occupancy during the redemption period. Entering the property constitutes criminal trespass.
- **Q: What is a "Redeemable Tax Deed" vs a "Standard Tax Deed"?**
  - **A: In a standard Tax Deed state (e.g., CA, FL, NV)**, the auction transfers direct, immediate ownership with no post-sale redemption. **In a Redeemable Deed state (e.g., TX, GA)**, you receive a recorded deed immediately, but the former owner has a statutory window (e.g., 6 months to 2 years) to buy it back by paying your entire purchase price plus a mandatory flat statutory penalty (e.g., 25% in Texas, 20% in Georgia).
- **Q: Can I lose my principal investment if the property owner never pays?**
  - **A: In a lien state, NO (provided you conduct basic due diligence)**. If the owner never pays, you have the statutory legal right to foreclose on the property and acquire the real estate. As long as the property's market value exceeds the back tax amount, your capital is protected by equity.

### 2. Bankruptcy & Legal Injunctions
- **Q: What happens if the property owner files Bankruptcy before or during the tax sale?**
  - **A: The Automatic Stay (§ 362 of U.S. Bankruptcy Code) immediately halts all foreclosure and tax sale actions.**
    - If filed *before* the auction: The sale is void ab initio. The county must refund your entire purchase price.
    - If filed *during the redemption period*: The redemption clock is temporarily tolled (frozen). The bankruptcy court will either require the debtor to pay the tax lien in full via a Chapter 13 repayment plan (with statutory interest) or lift the stay to allow you to complete foreclosure.

---

### 3. Encumbrances & Surviving Liens
- **Q: Does a tax deed sale wipe out an existing bank mortgage?**
  - **A: YES.** In all 50 states, validly noticed municipal tax liens are senior ("super-priority") to private mortgages, deeds of trust, promissory notes, mechanic's liens, and judgment liens. Provided the bank received constitutionally required statutory notice of the sale, their mortgage is extinguished.
- **Q: What specific liens SURVIVE a tax deed foreclosure?**
  - **A:**
    1. *IRS Federal Tax Liens*: Governed by 26 U.S.C. § 7425. The IRS has a 120-day right of redemption from the sale date. If the IRS redeems, they must pay you your full winning bid price plus 6% statutory interest.
    2. *Municipal Utility & Code Enforcement Liens*: City water, sewer, weed mowing, and emergency demolition assessments (varies by local county/city charter).
    3. *Special Assessment District Bonds (CDD / SID)*: Future ongoing bond infrastructure assessments.
    4. *Covenants & HOA Ongoing Dues*: Past delinquent HOA dues are wiped out in most states, but future ongoing covenants and monthly dues attach to the new title holder.

---

### 4. Property Possession & Eviction
- **Q: What do I do if someone is living in the home after I receive my Tax Deed?**
  - **A: NEVER use self-help eviction (changing locks or cutting power).** Follow a 2-step protocol:
    1. *Step 1: Friendly "Cash for Keys"*: Approach the occupant professionally. Offer $500–$2,000 cash in exchange for a signed surrender agreement and clean key handover within 10 days.
    2. *Step 2: Formal Writ of Possession / Ejectment*: If they refuse, file for a Writ of Possession with the county court. The sheriff will execute physical lockout.

---

### 5. Title Clearing & Insurance
- **Q: Why won't my local title insurance company issue a title policy on my new tax deed?**
  - **A: Title companies fear procedural notice defects in the county's pre-sale notifications.** To get standard title insurance, you must either:
    - File a judicial **Quiet Title Action** in county circuit court (3–6 months, $2,500–$4,000), OR
    - Hire a specialized certification agency like **Tax Title Services (TTS)** (30–60 days, $1,500–$2,000) to audit the notice records and issue a nationally recognized certification.

---

### 6. Taxes, Accounting & Subsequent Years
- **Q: What are "Subsequent Taxes" ("Subs"), and should I pay them?**
  - **A: YES.** If you hold a tax lien and the owner fails to pay taxes the following year, most counties give the existing certificate holder the first right to pay the "subs." The new payment is added to your existing certificate and earns the statutory interest rate without having to bid at auction.
- **Q: How are tax lien and deed profits taxed by the IRS?**
  - **A:**
    - *Tax Lien Interest*: Reported on Form 1099-INT and taxed as **ordinary income**.
    - *Tax Deed Real Estate Profits*: Taxed as short-term or long-term **capital gains** (reported on Form 1099-S).
    - *Tax-Free Alternative*: Holding investments inside a **Self-Directed Roth IRA** eliminates all federal and state taxes on both interest and capital gains.

## Anti-patterns & Common Pitfalls

- **Ignoring the 120-Day IRS Rule**: Selling or rehabbing a property within 120 days of auction without checking for recorded IRS federal tax liens.
- **Attempting Self-Help Eviction**: Changing deadbolts on an occupied tax deed home, triggering treble-damage lawsuits for unlawful lockout.
- **Letting Certificates Expire**: Forgetting to foreclose before statutory expiration dates (e.g., Florida liens expire after 7 years; Indiana after 1 year from sale).

---

## Key Takeaways

1. Liens convey zero rights of entry or occupancy during the redemption period; deeds convey ownership.
2. Private mortgages are extinguished by tax sales provided proper statutory notice was served.
3. Use Cash-for-Keys to resolve occupied properties quickly and avoid lengthy court evictions.
4. Clear title via Quiet Title Action or Tax Title Certification before attempting retail MLS resale.
5. SDIRAs and Solo 401(k)s shelter high statutory interest yields from ordinary income taxes.
