# Land Banks and Cheap Maryland Lots

An analysis of land banks (both governmental programs and private wholesale platforms) and the feasibility of finding building lots in Maryland priced under $10,000 that possess active percolation ("perc") approvals.

---

## What is a "Land Bank"?

In real estate, the term **Land Bank** has two distinct meanings:

1. **Governmental / Municipal Land Banks**: Public or non-profit entities authorized by state or local governments to acquire vacant, abandoned, and tax-foreclosed properties and return them to productive use.
2. **Private Land Wholesalers ("Land Banks")**: Private investment companies (e.g., LandCentury, Discount Lots, LandSpot) that buy cheap, distressed rural parcels in bulk off-market and resell them at a markup, often with owner financing.

## Best Land Bank & Surplus Sites for Maryland

### 1. Public Government Programs

#### 🟢 Buy Into Bmore (Baltimore City)
* **Website**: [Buy Into Bmore Portal](https://buyintobmore.baltimorecity.gov/)
* **Description**: Baltimore City's Department of Housing & Community Development (DHCD) manages thousands of city-owned vacant buildings and lots.
* **Pricing**: Lots are often available for under $10,000 (some as low as $1 to qualified buyers, community groups, or adjacent neighbors).
* **Perc Status**: **No perc testing is required** because these are urban infill lots served by municipal water and public sewer connections.
* **Catch**: Buyers must submit a formal redevelopment plan and prove they have the funds to maintain/develop the property.

#### 🟢 GovDeals (County & State Surplus)
* **Website**: [GovDeals Real Estate Auction](https://www.govdeals.com/)
* **Description**: A national online auction platform used by Maryland state agencies and county governments (e.g., Carroll, Prince George's, Somerset) to sell surplus land and tax-foreclosed properties.
* **Pricing**: Bids often start under $5,000.
* **Perc Status**: Sold strictly **"as-is"** with no utility guarantees. Active perc approvals are almost never provided; buyers must conduct their own due diligence during the bidding period.

## The Feasibility of Perc-Approved Lots Under $10k

> [!WARNING]
> **Reality Check: Finding a truly perc-approved buildable lot in Maryland under $10,000 is virtually impossible in the current market.**

### Why They Don't Exist:
1. **Entitlement Value**: The cost to schedule, perform, and secure a percolation test from a Maryland county health department, plus the cost of a boundary survey, is typically $3,000 to $7,000. Once a lot is officially certified as "perc-approved," its market value instantly jumps to at least **$20,000 to $30,000**, even in highly remote rural counties (such as Somerset or Garrett, as documented in [[Maryland Lots Under 30k]]).
2. **Flipper Arbitrage**: If a private wholesaler owns a lot and percs it successfully, they will sell it at market rate. If they are selling it for under $10k, it is almost certainly because they **have not tested it**, or they tested it and it **failed**, making it unbuildable.
3. **Strict Septic Regulations**: As outlined in [[Maryland Mind Map]], Maryland requires a primary septic field and a 10,000-square-foot reserve area. Cheap lots under $10k are usually too small (under 0.25 acres), steep, or wet to meet these criteria.

## Strategic Alternatives for Budget Buyers

If your budget is strictly under $10,000, consider these alternative strategies:

### 1. Infill Lots with Public Sewer (Municipal Programs)
Instead of looking for a perc lot, buy a cheap lot through **Buy Into Bmore** or local town surplus lists. These lots bypass the need for a septic system entirely because they connect to public sewer lines, eliminating perc risks.

### 2. Buy with a Perc Contingency Clause
If you find a rural lot for $8,000–$10,000 on Zillow or LandSearch:
* **Never buy it outright.**
* Submit an offer with a **perc contingency clause** giving you 90 days to test the soil.
* If the county health department approves the soil for septic, you close on the deal and instantly double your equity. If it fails, you walk away and get your deposit back.

### 3. Recreational Use Only
If you are willing to accept that the lot is unbuildable, you can buy unperc'd lots for $5,000 to $8,000 to use for camping, hunting, or solar placement (subject to local county zoning restrictions).
