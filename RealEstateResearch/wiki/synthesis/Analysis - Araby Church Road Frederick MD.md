### 2. Critical Development Roadblocks

* **Perc Test Status: FAILED (Not Approved)**: The listing records and public disclosures explicitly state that the property **previously failed a percolation test**. In Frederick County, where municipal sewer lines do not extend to this unincorporated area, a failed perc test means the Health Department cannot issue an On-site Sewage Disposal System (OSDS) permit. Without septic approval, the land is legally **unbuildable** for residential dwellings. Refer to [[Maryland Mind Map]] for details on Maryland's strict wet-season testing and reserve drainfield rules.
* **Lack of Utilities**: There is no water well on site. While electrical utility poles may be near the road, they cannot be utilized for residential building without a corresponding sanitary disposal solution.
* **Dual-Lot Discrepancy**: While the public records for the primary tax ID (`1107205597`) show 0.86 acres, the listing represents 1.72 acres because it packages two separate, adjacent 0.86-acre parcels (`1107205597` and `1107205589`) together. Both parcels are subject to the same failed perc constraints.
* **Access Limitations**: Access to the property is off Kidwiler Drive via Route 355 (Urbana area). Physical access, grading, and right-of-way easement verification would be required, though irrelevant unless the perc failure is resolved.

## Valuation & Investment Feasibility

To determine if this property is worth an offer, it must be evaluated against the local land dynamics of Frederick County:

1. **Comparison with Buildable Lots**:
   * Fully buildable, perc-approved residential lots of 1 to 2 acres in the Urbana/Frederick area typically command between **$150,000 and $220,000** depending on exact location and site preparation.
2. **Unbuildable Lot Discount**:
   * Vacant land that is legally unbuildable typically suffers an **85% to 95% discount** in market value. 
   * The utility of this property is restricted to:
     * Adjacent neighbors who wish to merge the parcels to expand their own lot boundaries, increase privacy, or create buffer zones.
     * Passive recreational activities (e.g., camping, keeping animals, agricultural buffers), subject to Frederick County R1 zoning restrictions.
3. **Speculative Redesign / Re-perc Feasibility**:
   * Attempting to "re-perc" a failed lot is highly speculative, costly, and has a low probability of success. It requires hiring a private-sector soil scientist and designer to perform deep trench testing, identifying alternative soil profiles, or applying for specialized sand mound/Best Available Technology (BAT) systems. 
   * Frederick County reviews these on a strict case-by-case basis, and standard R1 lots that have already failed are rarely salvaged without substantial soil engineering investments.
4. **Target Offer Pricing**:
   * If purchasing to resell to an adjacent neighbor or hold as a cheap speculative asset, the purchase price must reflect its unbuildable status.
   * The seller's asking price of $60,000 is highly unrealistic and has resulted in the property sitting on the market for **96 days** with zero buyers.
   * A reasonable, risk-adjusted speculative offer for this 1.72-acre package is **$5,000 to $8,000** total. This provides a safe margin to wholesale the contract or sell the parcels to neighboring landowners for $12,000 to $15,000.
