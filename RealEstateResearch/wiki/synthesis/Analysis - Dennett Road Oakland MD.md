# Analysis - Dennett Road Oakland MD

An investment and development feasibility analysis of the 10.10-acre residential land tract located on Dennett Road/Oakland Drive, Oakland, MD 21550 (MLS #MDGA2011252). This analysis evaluates whether the property is worth putting an offer on, considering its subdivision potential, utility access, and local market dynamics.

## Executive Recommendation

> [!TIP]
> **Recommendation: YES, this property is worth putting an offer on, but ONLY at a discounted price of $110,000 to $125,000 with a comprehensive due diligence study period.**

This lot represents a strong opportunity for a developer or land-flipper because it bypasses the most common roadblock in rural Maryland: **perc tests**. The availability of public sewer/water inside town limits, combined with pre-engineered drawings, makes this a high-margin play if acquired at the right price.

### 2. The Development Wins
* **Bypassing the Perc Test:** Most land tracts in Garrett County require private well and septic, which are highly restricted by percolation ("perc") testing and soil quality. Because this lot sits in Oakland town limits and has public sewer/water access, **septic failure risks are zero**.
* **Pre-Entitled Material:** The sale includes **engineered subdivision drawings** and a **grading permit** from the time of the previous survey. This saves $15,000 to $30,000 in upfront civil engineering costs and shortens the development timeline by 6–12 months.
* **Excellent Access:** The property features three separate cleared entrances from town roads (Dennett Road and Oakland Avenue), providing flexibility for roadway design and subdivision layouts.

## Why Has It Sat on the Market? (132 Days)

Despite its clear advantages, the property has not sold in over 4 months. An investor must understand these underlying challenges:

1. **High Infrastructure Costs (Civil Build-out)**:
   * Having water and sewer at the street is great, but extending lines, installing fire hydrants, building a paved road, putting in curbs/gutters, and constructing a stormwater management pond (to comply with Maryland's strict Environmental Site Design guidelines) is highly expensive.
   * Total development cost for a 20-lot subdivision in this terrain is estimated at **$30,000 to $50,000 per lot** ($600,000 to $1,000,000 total).
2. **Slow Absorption Rate (Oakland Market)**:
   * Oakland is a small, quiet municipal seat (pop. ~1,800) rather than a high-velocity resort market like Deep Creek Lake (McHenry). 
   * Local homebuyer demand for new-construction residential homes is low. Selling 20 new houses in this location might take **5 to 10 years**, creating a long holding period that eats developer profits through financing interest and taxes.
3. **Multi-Parcel Complexity**:
   * The sale involves 4 distinct parcels. Buyers must verify that all parcels are contiguous, have unified title, and have deeded access.

## Offer Strategy & Contingencies

To make this deal profitable, you must secure the land at a price that mitigates the long holding period and infrastructure costs:

### 1. Pricing Target
* **Target Offer Range**: **$110,000 to $125,000** (20% to 30% below asking).
* **Rationale**: At $120,000, the raw land cost is $6,000 per potential lot (assuming 20 lots). If civil infrastructure costs $40,000 per lot, the total finished lot cost is $46,000. Finished lots in this area can be sold to local custom builders for $60,000 to $75,000, leaving a healthy margin.

### 2. Mandatory Contract Contingencies
Any purchase contract must include a **60-to-90-day Feasibility Study Period** to verify:
* **Utility Allocation (Water/Sewer Taps)**: Confirm with the Town of Oakland that the utility system has 20 water and sewer taps available for allocation to this property and verify the exact tap connection fees.
* **Engineering Review**: Have a licensed civil engineer review the included subdivision drawings to ensure they comply with current municipal stormwater, environmental, and roadway codes.
* **Permit Status**: Confirm if the grading permit and preliminary subdivision plat are active or if they have expired and require re-submission.
* **Title & Survey**: Run a title search to ensure all 4 parcels have clear titles, no overlapping easements, and are fully contiguous.
