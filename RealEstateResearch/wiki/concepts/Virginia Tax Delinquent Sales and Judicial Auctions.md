# Virginia Tax Delinquent Sales and Judicial Auctions

A comprehensive guide explaining Virginia's property tax foreclosure process, the distinction between open-market MLS listings and municipal tax auctions, and how the **City of Chesapeake** executes judicial tax deed sales and surplus land auctions.

---

## ⚖️ Virginia Tax Foreclosure Framework: Tax Deed vs. Tax Lien

> [!NOTE]
> **Virginia is a Judicial Tax Deed Commonwealth, NOT a Tax Lien Certificate State.**
> In Virginia, local municipalities (such as the City of Chesapeake) **do not sell tax lien certificates** to private investors for interest returns. Instead, when real estate taxes remain unpaid, the city initiates a **Judicial Foreclosure Lawsuit** pursuant to **Virginia Code § 58.1-3965 et seq.** to sell the physical real estate via a Special Commissioner's Tax Deed at public auction.

### Key Legal Rules in Virginia:
1. **Delinquency Threshold**: Real estate taxes must typically be delinquent on December 31 following the second or third anniversary of the date on which taxes became due before judicial foreclosure is filed.
2. **Circuit Court Confirmation**: Sales are conducted through the Circuit Court of the City of Chesapeake. The winning bid is subject to final judicial confirmation by a Circuit Court judge before the Special Commissioner issues a **Special Warranty Deed**.
3. **Right of Redemption**: The delinquent property owner retains the statutory right to redeem the property by paying all accrued taxes, penalties, interest, and legal costs up until the moment the hammer falls at the auction (or prior to court confirmation).

## 🔍 Market Status: Open MLS Listings vs. Tax Auction Properties

### 1. Active Open-Market MLS Lots (Private Sale)
* Properties such as **[[Analysis - 1813 Shillelagh Road Chesapeake VA]]** ($290,000) and **[[Analysis - 1836 Mount Pleasant Road Chesapeake VA]]** ($250,000) are **standard private market listings**, NOT tax lien or judicial tax auction properties.
* They are owned by private individuals/entities, listed through licensed real estate brokerages on the regional MLS (REIN MLS), convey via standard General Warranty Deed, and have clear title procedures.

### 2. City of Chesapeake Judicial Tax Sales (Tax Deed Auctions)
* Delinquent properties undergoing judicial foreclosure are sold via periodic public auctions administered by the City Treasurer and contracted Special Commissioners / Auctioneers (such as Virginia Auction Co. or Taxing Authority Consulting Services - TACS).
* Features of Judicial Tax Sale Parcels:
  * Sold "As-Is, Where-Is" with no warranties regarding buildability, soil perc suitability, or environmental condition.
  * Often consist of landlocked parcels, slivers, or properties with complex title clouds.
  * Require 100% cash settlement or certified funds deposit upon auction completion.

### 3. City of Chesapeake Surplus Land Sales
* Separate from delinquent tax foreclosures, the City of Chesapeake occasionally disposes of surplus government-owned real estate under **Chesapeake City Code § 54-1 et seq.**. These auctions are published on the official City of Chesapeake procurement/surplus portal.
