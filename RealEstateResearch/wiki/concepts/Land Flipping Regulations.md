# Land Flipping Regulations

This page covers the legal and licensing requirements for buying, entitlement-processing, and selling (flipping) land in the State of Maryland.

---

## Do You Need a License to Buy Land?

**No, you do not need any license to buy land.** 
As an individual, partnership, trust, or corporation (such as an LLC), you have the right to purchase real estate directly from a seller. You can handle the transaction yourself, or hire a licensed real estate agent to represent you.

---

## Do You Need a License to Sell Land You Own?

**No, you do not need a license to sell land that you legally own.**
Under the "For Sale By Owner" (FSBO) exception in property law, any property owner has the right to sell their own real estate asset without a real estate broker's or agent's license. This applies to both raw land and developed lots.

* **Flipping Play**: If you purchase the land, close the transaction, take title (either personally or through an LLC), and then sell it to a builder, this is a standard **buy-and-sell flip**. No license is required.

---

## Do You Need a License to Wholesale (Assign Contracts)?

> [!WARNING]
> **Wholesaling (assigning contracts) without a license is heavily restricted in Maryland.**

If your strategy is to put a property under contract and then "assign" that contract to a builder for a fee (without ever actually buying and closing on the property yourself):

* **Maryland Senate Bill 975**: Maryland law explicitly targets unlicensed wholesaling. The law states that "wholesaling"—defined as marketing or selling an equitable interest in a real estate contract—is considered a real estate brokerage activity.
* **The Rule**: You **must** be a licensed real estate salesperson or broker in Maryland to market or sell contract assignments to third-party buyers (like builders) for a fee. Doing so without a license can lead to severe fines and misdemeanor charges from the Maryland Real Estate Commission (MREC).

---

## Safe Flipping Strategies Without a License

If you do not have a real estate license, you should use the following strategies to flip land to builders legally:

### 1. The Standard Buy-and-Sell (Double Close)
* **The Process**: You sign a contract to purchase the lot, perform your due diligence (perc testing, utility checks), and **close on the purchase** using your own cash, transactional funding, or a hard money loan. Once you own the deed, you can re-list it and sell it to a builder.
* **Why it's safe**: Because you are the actual legal owner of the property when you sell it, you do not need a license.

### 2. Entity Buying (LLCs)
* **The Process**: Form a Maryland **Limited Liability Company (LLC)** for your land-flipping business. Purchase all lots under the LLC. 
* **Why it works**: Using an LLC protects your personal assets from any liability (such as environmental issues on the land or dispute claims from the builder).
* **Using a Non-Real-Estate LLC (e.g., Computer Systems Design)**:
  * **Legally Permissible**: **Yes, you can legally do this.** Under Maryland corporate law, unless your LLC's operating agreement specifically forbids it, your LLC has the power to acquire, hold, mortgage, and sell real estate, regardless of its primary business purpose.
  * **Cross-Liability Risk (Not Recommended)**: While legal, mixing different business activities in a single LLC is highly risky:
    * > [!CAUTION]
      > **Assets at Risk**: If your Computer Systems Design business is sued by an IT client (for data loss, breach of contract, etc.), the land owned by that LLC is a corporate asset and can be seized or sold to pay court judgments.
    * > [!CAUTION]
      > **Land Liability**: If someone gets injured on the land, or you face environmental violations (e.g., runoff in a protected watershed), your IT business revenues and client contracts inside that LLC are exposed to the lawsuit.
  * **Bank & Financing Complications**: If you seek a commercial loan or hard money to purchase the land, commercial lenders prefer **Single Purpose Entities (SPEs)**—LLCs whose sole purpose is holding that specific piece of real estate. Mixing active service revenue with real estate assets complicates credit underwriting and tax accounting.
  * **Best Practice**: Create a **separate, dedicated real estate LLC** (e.g., "XYZ Land Holdings LLC") to keep your IT consulting business and your land investments completely insulated from one another. In Maryland, the cost of registering a second LLC is minor compared to the asset-protection value it offers.

### 3. Hiring a Licensed Agent to List the Property
* **The Process**: Once you own the property and have added value (e.g., secured the active perc approval), hire a licensed Maryland land broker to list the property on the MLS and market it to their builder network.
* **Why it works**: You pay a commission (typically 5–10% for land), but the agent handles all legal marketing compliance and exposes the lot to a much larger pool of builders.
