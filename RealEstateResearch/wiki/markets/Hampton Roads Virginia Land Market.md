# Hampton Roads Virginia Land Market (Chesapeake, Hampton, Yorktown, Poquoson)

A comprehensive market overview of residential vacant land, utility infrastructure, perc regulations, and development dynamics across the four target submarkets in Southeastern Virginia: **City of Chesapeake**, **City of Hampton**, **Yorktown (York County)**, and **City of Poquoson**.

---

## 🗺️ Regional Submarket Breakdown

### 1. City of Chesapeake (Southside)
* **Zoning Classifications**:
  * `R-15S` / `R-15`: Residential Suburban (minimum lot size 15,000 sq ft). Single-family detached homes.
  * `A-1`: Agricultural District (minimum lot size 3 acres). Preserves rural and farming character; allows single-family homes with on-site septic.
* **Utility Landscape**:
  * **North (Deep Creek, Western Branch, Greenbrier)**: Full municipal water and sanitary sewer operated by the City of Chesapeake Department of Public Utilities.
  * **South (Hickory, Mount Pleasant, Grassfield)**: Governed by the Rural Overlay Zone. City policy intentionally limits municipal sewer line extensions southward to curb suburban sprawl. Developments rely on private wells and Virginia Department of Health (VDH) certified on-site sewage disposal systems (conventional or alternative engineered septic).
* **Soil & Perc Characteristics**:
  * Low water table in southern wetlands (Dismal Swamp proximity) necessitates strict seasonal water table monitoring and engineered mound or drip dispersal systems for lots without conventional perc.

### 2. City of Hampton (Peninsula)
* **Zoning Classifications**:
  * `R-13` / `R-9`: Single-family residential districts requiring 13,000 and 9,000 sq ft minimums.
  * `L-R`: Low-Density Residential.
* **Utility Landscape**:
  * Fully urbanized infrastructure. Water is supplied by **Newport News Waterworks**, and sewage collection/treatment is handled by the **Hampton Roads Sanitation District (HRSD)** and City of Hampton Public Works.
  * Infill parcels almost universally have tap connections at the street right-of-way.
* **Environmental & Coastal Overlays**:
  * Waterfront properties (e.g., Salt Ponds, Long Creek, Fox Hill) fall under the **Chesapeake Bay Preservation Act (CBPA)** with mandatory 100-foot **Resource Protection Area (RPA)** vegetative buffer restrictions.

### 3. Yorktown / York County (Peninsula)
* **Zoning Classifications**:
  * `RR`: Rural Residential (minimum 1 acre).
  * `RC`: Resource Conservation (minimum 5 acres for standard subdivisions, low-density single-family).
  * `R-20` / `R-13`: Single-Family Suburban Residential.
* **Utility Landscape**:
  * High-density corridors (Route 17 / George Washington Memorial Hwy, Ship Point, Dare) are tied into county water lines (Newport News Waterworks) and York County Environmental Services gravity sewer mains.
  * Secondary rural tracts (Railway Rd, Kirby Ln, Seaford) utilize VDH certified septic drainfields with private or county water.
* **Key Development Value**:
  * York County requires lower property tax rates than neighboring independent cities and commands high resale demand due to the York County School Division.

### 4. City of Poquoson (Peninsula)
* **Zoning Classifications**:
  * `R-1`: Low-Density Single-Family Residential (minimum lot area typically 20,000 sq ft).
  * `R-2`: Medium-Density Single-Family Residential.
* **Utility Landscape**:
  * City of Poquoson Public Works maintains sewer collection lines routing to HRSD regional treatment facilities. Potable water is supplied by Newport News Waterworks.
  * Due to limited land supply and top-tier statewide school rankings, vacant buildable parcels trade at a significant premium per acre ($80,000–$150,000/acre).
